# Greenland Faction Unit Rosters

## One-Line Summary

The first-campaign factions still make sense if their units are not generic soldiers in different colors, but expressions of what each faction believes survival should become: local sovereignty, proprietary climate maintenance, certified polar control, treaty peacekeeping, atmospheric commons, concession logistics, or weaponized biosecurity.

## Faction Fit Verdict

Yes, the current faction set still makes sense. The strongest playable core is:

1. **Home Rule Coalition** — local legitimacy, terrain knowledge, rescue, community defense, consent politics.
2. **QXZ Meridian Arctic Mandate** — White Sky maintenance, climate liability, proprietary rescue, elite infrastructure security.
3. **Barents Research Group / Polar Certification Combine** — polar data, route certification, sensors, drones, “neutral” scientific control.
4. **Digital Peacekeeping Directorate / the Blue / the Blues** — Treaty Net, public infrastructure, cyber peacekeeping, mandate-limited force.

The best antagonist/side-faction layer is:

5. **Open Sky / Blue Week Cells** — atmospheric commons, leaks, sabotage, direct action, risky truth.
6. **Janus-Kestrel Mining-Logistics Consortium** — ports, cargo identity, extraction finance, private security, concession contracts.
7. **Tundra Biosecurity / Wildlife Control Program** — better as neutral dwelling chain or subfaction first; full faction later if the animal-control fantasy proves strong.

## Cultural Naming Note

Avoid using **Kalaallit** as a faction brand. It is the real endonym connected to Greenlandic national and cultural identity, and using it as a playable strategy-faction label risks turning a living people into game IP.

Safer current placeholder: **Home Rule Coalition**. It describes a political formation in the fiction without claiming to represent all Greenlandic people or appropriating the endonym as a brand. Real Greenland / Kalaallit Nunaat can still be treated respectfully as a place with local sovereignty, municipalities, languages, communities, and interests.

## Shared Unit Design Rules

- Units are “creatures” in HoMM terms, but diegetically they are squads, drone groups, specialist teams, animals, platform swarms, or contracted crews.
- Avoid pure soldier reskins. Every unit line should imply a source building, social class, legal status, and upgrade path.
- Tactical battles should allow ranged fire, cover, terrain, hacking, morale/publicity effects, extraction objectives, and Intel preservation.
- Upgrades should look like better doctrine, better equipment, stronger legal mandate, cleaner feed access, or more exploitative contracts — not fantasy evolution.
- Elite units should be ethically or politically expensive, not just stronger.

## Roster Scale Proposal

For MVP, each playable faction needs only:
- 3 recruitable unit lines;
- 1 Champion;
- 1 neutral/shared recruit line;
- 1 signature tactical mechanic.

For vertical slice, expand each faction to:
- 6-7 unit lines, HoMM-style;
- basic and upgraded variants;
- one faction-specific recruitment site;
- one elite or controversial unit.

## Home Rule Coalition

### Fantasy

Local sovereignty under pressure. The faction wins by knowing the land, protecting communities, controlling legitimacy, and making outsiders pay for every shortcut.

### Visual Identity

Weathered civic gear, sealift containers, municipal rescue orange, hunter/fisher equipment, patched drones, sled/ATV logistics, community mesh radios, snow camouflage, old Danish/Greenlandic/UN markings repainted by local councils.

### Tactical Identity

Defensive mobility, terrain bonuses, rescue/sustain, ambushes, legitimacy/proof generation, lower raw tech but better Arctic adaptation.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Settlement Watch | Council Guard | Defensive infantry | Local militia, good in settlements/outposts, weak in open elite fights. |
| 2 | Sled Logistics Team | Whiteout Couriers | Support/mobility | Improves movement, extraction, supply, can evacuate wounded or Intel. |
| 3 | Hunter-Scouts | Fjord Stalkers | Recon/skirmisher | Terrain stealth, marks targets, strong in snow/fjord maps. |
| 4 | SAR Drone Crew | Rescue Mesh Wing | Support drones | Heals/stabilizes, reveals terrain, can recover proof from battlefield. |
| 5 | Fishery Hardhats | Harbor Defense Union | Tough melee/riot line | Industrial workers with exosuits/tools; strong near ports/factories. |
| 6 | Compact Negotiators | Consent Wardens | Soft-control/support | Buffs allies, debuffs corporate units, generates Proof/Legitimacy on objectives. |
| 7 | Heritage Ranger Team | Civic Vanguard | Elite adaptive squad | High morale, terrain mastery, anti-drone tools; politically costly if lost. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Consent Network** — controlling civic sites gives defensive buffs and Proof. Destroying local infrastructure weakens this faction but damages the attacker’s legitimacy.

### Strongest Unit Ideas

- **Fjord Stalkers**: scouts who can disappear in bad weather and mark enemy units for ambush.
- **Consent Wardens**: not fantasy priests; lawyers, elders, translators, feed witnesses, and negotiators who turn legitimacy into tactical effects.
- **Civic Vanguard**: elite local defenders, not super-soldiers; their power is competence, local trust, and terrain.

## QXZ Meridian Arctic Mandate

### Fantasy

Proprietary life support. QXZ believes climate stability is too fragile for democratic improvisation and that White Sky infrastructure must be protected even when people hate it.

### Visual Identity

White/pearl corporate armor, pale-blue hazard lights, clean sealed suits, atmospheric maintenance logos, stratospheric sensor drones, climate-service vehicles, sterile clinics, liability seals, mirrored visors reflecting a white sky.

### Tactical Identity

High-tech combined arms, hazard control, shields/barriers, climate-station buffs, expensive elite units, strong defensive infrastructure, weak local legitimacy.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meridian Security | Mandate Response Team | Infantry | Clean corporate security, disciplined, costly. |
| 2 | Aerosol Techs | Skyline Engineers | Support/repair | Repairs drones/structures, deploys cover, stabilizes climate hazards. |
| 3 | Liability Medics | Continuity Clinicians | Heal/cleanse | Medical support with insurance/protocol flavor; may “stabilize” enemies for capture. |
| 4 | Strato Sensor Swarm | Meridian Eye Swarm | Recon/drone | Reveals stealth, marks targets, improves ranged accuracy. |
| 5 | Climate Bulwark | Albedo Bastion | Heavy defender | Shield unit, area denial, protects infrastructure. |
| 6 | Termination-Risk Team | Emergency Suppression Unit | Control/anti-sabotage | Locks down zones, counters Open Sky/Free-Net actions. |
| 7 | White Sky Custodians | Meridian Paladins | Elite | Iconic corporate knight analogue; terrifying because they really may be preventing catastrophe. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Stability Protocols** — QXZ can reduce environmental hazards and protect infrastructure, but some actions increase Public Distrust or local resistance.

### Strongest Unit Ideas

- **Albedo Bastion**: a mobile shield/maintenance platform — the “tower unit” that fits tactical battles.
- **Termination-Risk Team**: emergency force justified by catastrophe modeling; tactically strong, politically ugly.
- **Meridian Paladins**: not literal paladins; elite climate-infrastructure guardians with almost religious PR framing.

## Barents Research Group / Polar Certification Combine

### Fantasy

Whoever certifies the Arctic owns the Arctic. Barents does not need to conquer territory openly; it controls maps, risk scores, route permissions, sensor truth, and rescue data.

### Visual Identity

Research orange, oceanic teal, black sensor fabric, expedition frames, autonomous buoys, ice radar, lab coats under armor, branded “safety” tags, neutral scientific typography that hides corporate teeth.

### Tactical Identity

Scouting, targeting, route control, drones, debuffs through sensor superiority, strong map knowledge, medium combat durability.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Field Surveyors | Certified Route Team | Scout/support | Finds hidden paths and hazards; weak combat. |
| 2 | Ice Radar Operators | Cryo-Mapping Cell | Reveal/debuff | Exposes stealth, reduces enemy mobility. |
| 3 | Research Drones | Boreal Sensor Net | Drone swarm | Cheap recon/harass; enables precision attacks. |
| 4 | Rescue Contractors | Denied-Zone Responders | Mobile infantry | SAR with private-security edge. |
| 5 | Cable Divers | Under-Ice Saboteurs | Specialist | Coastal/under-ice maps; disables Net/energy nodes. |
| 6 | Risk Actuaries | Certification Board | Command/support | Rewrites tactical risk: buffs allies, debuffs enemies, manipulates initiative. |
| 7 | Polar Exclusion Team | Black-Ice Wardens | Elite control squad | Area denial, sensor dominance, anti-stealth/anti-drone. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Certified Route** — Barents can mark safe/unsafe routes. In battle, it can designate zones as “certified” for ally bonuses or “excluded” for enemy penalties.

### Strongest Unit Ideas

- **Risk Actuaries**: ridiculous name on purpose, but cyberpunk-strong — people whose models decide who is allowed to move.
- **Cable Divers**: great Greenland-specific unit; ports, cables, under-ice infrastructure, sabotage.
- **Black-Ice Wardens**: elite polar cyber-security / physical exclusion team; name also nods to cyberpunk ICE.

## Digital Peacekeeping Directorate — the Blue / the Blues

### Fantasy

Peacekeeping in a networked world where public access, hospital routing, evidence chains, and AGI quarantine are battlefields. The Blues are necessary, distrusted, mandate-bound, and always late until suddenly they are everywhere.

### Visual Identity

UN blue helmets translated into AR/cyber gear: blue bands, blue-lane overlays, white vehicles, humanitarian markings, hard legal labels, transparent shields, court-admissible bodycams, portable relay masts.

### Tactical Identity

Defensive control, nonlethal options, anti-hack, proof stabilization, emergency routing, strong objective play, limited aggression unless mandate conditions are met.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blue Line Operators | Treaty Net Guards | Basic infantry/security | Protects nodes, modest combat, strong near civic infrastructure. |
| 2 | Civic Relay Techs | Blue-Lane Engineers | Support | Deploys relays, restores comms, repairs Net objectives. |
| 3 | Mandate Observers | Evidentiary Officers | Proof/support | Records actions, protects evidence, punishes unlawful attacks. |
| 4 | Cyber Peacekeepers | Quarantine Breach Team | Anti-hack specialist | Counters Digital-Net actions and rogue systems. |
| 5 | Humanitarian Convoy | Protected Relief Column | Sustain/objective | Heals, supplies, shields civilians; slow but hard to ignore. |
| 6 | Blue Shield Squad | Directorate Intervention Unit | Control infantry | Riot/nonlethal tools, shields, zone control. |
| 7 | AGI Compact Wardens | Blue Mandate Executors | Elite | Anti-rogue-model / quarantine unit; legally terrifying when unleashed. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Mandate Threshold** — some powerful Blue actions unlock only after treaty violations, civilian danger, Net collapse, or AGI-quarantine risk is documented.

### Strongest Unit Ideas

- **Evidentiary Officers**: they make battlefield actions legally real; very Neon Champions.
- **Protected Relief Column**: a “caravan” unit that is tactically awkward but strategically valuable.
- **Blue Mandate Executors**: elite force that feels scary because it arrives with legal inevitability.

## Open Sky / Blue Week Cells

### Fantasy

The sky belongs to everyone, or no one survives with dignity. Open Sky is part activist network, part leak collective, part sabotage ecology, part desperate public conscience.

### Visual Identity

Patchwork cold-weather clothes, anti-corporate blue-sky symbols, pirate AR paint, cheap drones, repurposed climbing gear, solar scraps, printed masks, hand-painted slogans over hacked corporate equipment.

### Tactical Identity

Ambush, sabotage, mobility, morale shocks, leaks, fragile units with high disruption potential, strong against infrastructure and public narrative.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Protest Line | Direct Action Bloc | Cheap infantry/control | Weak damage, can block/occupy objectives. |
| 2 | Free-Net Runners | Pirate Relay Ghosts | Hacker/scout | Moves through Net layer, reveals hidden proof. |
| 3 | Banner Drones | Sky Graffiti Swarm | Morale/media | Debuffs enemy legitimacy, spreads visibility. |
| 4 | Climber-Saboteurs | Meridian Cutters | Infrastructure damage | Strong vs stations, relays, vehicles. |
| 5 | Leak Lawyers | Commons Advocates | Support/control | Turns captured Intel/Proof into public penalties. |
| 6 | Weather Hack Cell | Blue Break Cell | Risky special | Can trigger environmental chaos; may backfire. |
| 7 | Unceiling Vanguard | Open Sky Martyrs | Elite irregular | High disruption, morale effect, politically explosive casualties. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Make It Public** — Open Sky can turn battlefield events into Proof/Legitimacy swings. The more visible the fight, the more dangerous they become.

### Pushback

Open Sky should not be the default “good rebels.” Some cells are reckless. Some are infiltrated. Some cause real harm. Their moral force comes from being partly right and tactically dangerous.

## Janus-Kestrel Mining-Logistics Consortium

### Fantasy

Development as capture. Janus-Kestrel wins by making every port, road, cargo tag, payroll, and mineral claim pass through its systems.

### Visual Identity

Industrial yellow/black, port authority marks, container geometry, cargo drones, private security matte armor, mining exosuits, finance-contract UI, customs seals, logistics barcodes.

### Tactical Identity

Build tempo, armored workers, private security, logistics buffs, resource extraction, contract penalties, strong economy but vulnerable to legitimacy attacks.

### Unit Lines

| Tier | Basic Unit | Upgraded Unit | Role | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site Security | Concession Guards | Infantry | Cheap private guards tied to claims. |
| 2 | Cargo Handlers | Bonded Logistics Crew | Support | Improves movement/supply; can fortify objectives. |
| 3 | Prospecting Teams | Claim Engineers | Economy/scout | Marks resource sites; weak combat. |
| 4 | Mining Exosuits | Heavy Extraction Frames | Heavy melee/utility | Durable, can damage terrain/cover. |
| 5 | Customs Brokers | Contract Enforcers | Control/support | Slows enemy movement, taxes routes, manipulates objectives. |
| 6 | Port Drones | Kestrel Cargo Swarm | Ranged/drone | Mobile ranged pressure; stronger near supply lines. |
| 7 | Concession Breakers | Janus Black Ledger Unit | Elite | Private military/accounting hybrid; punishes enemies economically and tactically. |

### Signature Mechanic

**Concession Contract** — Janus-Kestrel can mark sites as concession zones, gaining resource advantages and imposing movement/tax penalties, but increasing local resistance and Open Sky/local hostility.

## Tundra Biosecurity / Wildlife Control Program

### Recommended Role

Start as a neutral dwelling chain, scenario hazard, or subfaction. It is cool, but making it a full faction too early risks tonal silliness unless grounded hard.

### Fantasy

Wildlife management turned into command infrastructure. The horror is not animal magic; it is telemetry, stress ecology, disease monitoring, drones, collars, and bioacoustic coercion turning animals into a contested security layer.

### Unit / Dwelling Ideas

| Site | Recruit / Effect | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Wildlife Patrol Station | Sled Dog Logistics Team | Movement/rescue support |
| Bioacoustic Tower | Musk Ox Stampede Rig | Area denial / disruption |
| Raven Tagging Lab | Raven Sensor Swarm | Scouting / signal spoofing |
| Polar Bear Collar Facility | Polar Bear Bio-Tag Alpha | Rare elite beast / ethical scandal |
| Coastal Buoy Nursery | Seal/Narwhal Buoy Network | Coastal detection / route control |
| Quarantine Blind | Parasite Panic Event | Scenario hazard, not normal recruit |

### Pushback

Use polar bears sparingly. They should be rare, frightening, and reputationally radioactive — not a standard cavalry unit.

## Tactical Battle Feel

A Neon Champions tactical battle should not look like two modern armies lining up. It should look like a contested site:

- fish plant loading yard;
- radar dome service road;
- ice runway;
- hydro intake tunnel;
- server bunker;
- port container stack;
- White Sky calibration mast;
- evacuated settlement street;
- under-ice cable landing.

Objects matter:
- server racks can be captured for Intel;
- relay masts can change Net visibility;
- civilians/proof witnesses can constrain attacks;
- climate equipment can create hazards;
- drones can be hacked instead of destroyed;
- extracting a VIP may matter more than wiping enemies.

## MVP Recommendation

For the first tactical prototype, use two factions and six units total:

### Home Rule Coalition
- Settlement Watch / Council Guard
- Hunter-Scouts / Fjord Stalkers
- SAR Drone Crew / Rescue Mesh Wing

### QXZ Meridian
- Meridian Security / Mandate Response Team
- Aerosol Techs / Skyline Engineers
- Climate Bulwark / Albedo Bastion

Scenario: contested White Sky sensor station near a fishery/airstrip. Optional Intel objective: capture the server rack before QXZ wipes it or before local systems collapse.

## Open Questions

- Should unit tiers mirror HoMM exactly, or use fewer broader tactical roles?
- Are upgraded variants permanent building unlocks, Intel upgrades, or both?
- Do Champions command stacks like HoMM, squads on a grid, or hybrid formations?
- Should nonlethal capture be a general mechanic or mostly a Blue/Kalaallit feature?
- How many units can fit tactically before combat becomes too slow?
