# Game Pillars

## Pillars

### Pillar: Strategic Clarity Under Dirty Information

Definition: The player should make readable strategic choices in a world where information is partial, polluted, source-tagged, or politically shaped.

Design test: If choosing between hidden randomness and source-tagged uncertainty, choose source-tagged uncertainty because the player must be able to reason about risk.

Served by: scouting states, Digital-Net overlay, Proof, Intel, source tags, rival propaganda, Axiom/UNP/corporate admissibility states.

### Pillar: Infrastructure Is Power

Definition: Towns, stations, networks, concessions, climate systems, ports, fisheries, mines, and proof layers should be as strategically meaningful as armies.

Design test: If capturing a site is only income-per-turn, add social, informational, logistical, legal, or crisis consequences.

Served by: hubs, outposts, White Sky nodes, Treaty Net routes, fishery/cold-chain sites, mines, clinics, ports, air hubs, crisis clocks.

### Pillar: Champions as Command, Identity, and Legitimacy

Definition: Champions are strategic commanders and public symbols whose bodies, reputations, legality, death states, and media narratives matter.

Design test: If a Champion is only a stronger unit, redesign them until they affect command, legitimacy, assets, doctrine, and narrative risk.

Served by: Champion assets, Intel upgrades, public legitimacy, death/capture/Echo states, faction doctrines, media/proof systems.

### Pillar: Factions as Evolutionary Philosophies

Definition: Each faction expresses a distinct answer to what humans, bodies, institutions, and intelligence should become.

Design test: If two factions differ only by units or aesthetics, redesign them until their strategic incentives and worldview differ.

Served by: faction buildings, unit lines, resource preferences, Intel use, body tech, Net access, legitimacy rules, crisis responses.

### Pillar: Wilderness Without Escapism

Definition: The first campaign should recover the wonder and danger of fantasy wilderness through grounded cyberpunk Arctic distance, weather, animals, logistics, and small outposts with global stakes. Its unnamed fictional region is internally Greenland-inspired without being publicly identified as Greenland.

Design test: If wilderness becomes empty scenery, turn it into route risk, sensor uncertainty, local knowledge, convoy cost, survival infrastructure, or moral pressure.

Served by: Arctic routes, weather events, isolated hubs, wilderness patrols, wildlife systems, air/sea mobility, local consent, search-and-rescue.

### Pillar: Intel Turns Secrets Into Power

Definition: The special upgrade resource should feel like stolen research, proofs, blackmail, model leaks, and operational knowledge becoming concrete advantage.

Design test: If Intel could be renamed mana or dust without changing the fiction, the design has failed.

Served by: asset upgrades, proof nodes, heists, reverse engineering, rival Champion scandals, environmental-model leaks, dark-net caches.

## Anti-Pillars

### Anti-Pillar: No Direct Magic

Fantasy functions must be translated into cyberpunk systems, institutions, biotech, command tech, information warfare, drones, legal/provenance actions, or climate/compute infrastructure.

### Anti-Pillar: No Generic Nation-vs-Corp Map

The map should preserve recognizable states and places but make lived power visible through overlays: maintenance, climate service, concessions, corridors, provenance, logistics, and crisis clocks.

### Anti-Pillar: No Clean UNP World Government

The UNP can coordinate Treaty Net and AGI Compact systems, but it cannot solve corporate capture, private grids, dark nets, pirate meshes, or political conflict.

### Anti-Pillar: No Fertile-Arctic Fantasy

The first region should be newly accessible and contested, not magically transformed into a broad agricultural paradise. Food systems are fisheries, greenhouses, cold-chain logistics, nutrients, and symbolic green zones.

### Anti-Pillar: No Agent-Authored Design Drift

Agents may propose and critique but may not silently turn exploratory ideas into approved production requirements.
