# Game Pillars

## Pillar candidates

These are placeholders pending formal concept work.

### Pillar: Strategic Clarity Under Dirty Information

Definition: The player should make readable strategic choices in a world where information is partial, polluted, or politically shaped.

Design test: If choosing between hidden randomness and source-tagged uncertainty, choose source-tagged uncertainty because the player must be able to reason about risk.

### Pillar: Factions as Evolutionary Philosophies

Definition: Each faction expresses a distinct answer to what humans, bodies, institutions, and intelligence should become.

Design test: If two factions differ only by units or aesthetics, redesign them until their strategic incentives and worldview differ.

### Pillar: Champions as Command, Identity, and Legitimacy

Definition: Champions are strategic commanders whose bodies, reputations, legality, death states, and public narratives matter.

Design test: If a Champion is only a stronger unit, the design has failed.

### Pillar: Infrastructure Is Power

Definition: Towns, stations, networks, concessions, climate systems, and proof layers should be as strategically meaningful as armies.

Design test: If capturing a site is just income-per-turn, add social, informational, logistical, or crisis consequences.

## Anti-pillar candidates

### Anti-Pillar: No direct magic

Fantasy functions must be translated into cyberpunk systems, institutions, biotech, command tech, information warfare, or climate/compute infrastructure.

### Anti-Pillar: No generic nation-vs-corp map

The map should preserve recognizable states but make lived power visible through overlays: maintenance, climate service, concessions, corridors, provenance, and crisis clocks.

### Anti-Pillar: No agent-authored design drift

Agents may propose and critique but may not silently turn exploratory ideas into production requirements.
